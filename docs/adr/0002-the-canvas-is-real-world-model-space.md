# The canvas is real-world model space, and sheets are placed at their drawing scale

Zach asked for a canvas he can "zoom way out" on, "like in AutoCAD model view", with PDFs placed "in the dimensions they would print in". We considered a canvas measured in printed inches, where every sheet sits at its paper size side by side, and chose a canvas measured in real-world lengths instead, as AutoCAD's model space is. A sheet is placed at its drawing scale, so a 1/4" = 1'-0" sheet of a 24 × 36 drawing covers 96 × 144 feet, and ink drawn over it is in the same real units as the plant it shows. Measures and dimensions read real lengths directly.

## Consequences

- Sheets at different drawing scales come out at very different sizes on one canvas. That is the model working, not a bug.
- Every sheet needs a drawing scale before it is placed. Vellum reads the SCALE from the PDF's title block and Zach confirms it. When there is none it can read (a scan, "AS NOTED", several scales on one sheet), Vellum asks him to type one, offering common scales.
- A sheet that is not to scale is an NTS sheet: placed at its printed size, its lengths shown as printed lengths marked NTS.
- Each canvas chooses its units: feet and inches, decimal feet or metric. A new canvas starts in feet and inches.
- A frame (a paper size at a drawing scale) is how a region of model space becomes one printable, sendable sheet again, as a layout viewport does in AutoCAD.
- "Print size" means a sheet prints at its paper size when it is printed at its drawing scale.

## Amendment (#8): measures, dimensions and frames read the model

Zach's picks from the prototype at the tag `prototype/measure-frame` settle how lengths are read and regions framed:

- **Lengths round in the canvas units.** Feet and inches round to the nearest 1/16" (Zach picked 1/16" over 1/2", 1/4" and 1"); decimal feet and metric stay as the canvas units already set them (hundredths of a foot, millimetres). A measure or dimension with either point on an NTS sheet reads printed inches marked NTS in every canvas units (`Canvas.measure`). A dimension's length is never stored: it is read from its points whenever it is drawn, so changing the canvas units changes it.
- **A dimension is pulled off, the AutoCAD way.** Two taps, then a third point: above or below the two points reads the horizontal distance (DIMLINEAR), beside them the vertical, out at an angle or between them the straight distance (DIMALIGNED). Its points are kept on the ink grid, so it reopens exactly.
- **A frame is a dragged box.** Zach picks the paper (8.5 × 11, 11 × 17, 24 × 36 or 30 × 42); the paper is turned the way the drag is, and the frame takes the largest standard drawing scale whose paper still covers the drag (the fewest real inches to the printed inch), centred on it (`Frame.fitting`). The scales tried are full size, the architectural scales and the engineering scales on a feet canvas, and 1:1 to 1:5000 in 1-2-5 steps on a metric one. A window offers "Make a frame here", which fits a frame to it the same way.

## Amendment (#11): sheets are placed, and a sheet can hold scale areas

Zach's picks from the prototype at the tag `prototype/place-sheets` settle how a PDF page becomes a sheet:

- **One drawing scale per sheet no longer holds.** An AS NOTED sheet, or one with several scales, goes on as one sheet at its main scale (the title block's, or the first scale written under a detail), which sets its size on the canvas. Every detail written at another scale becomes a **scale area** (CONTEXT.md): Vellum outlines it, and inside it Measure and Dimension read the area's scale, as the printed length the two points span on the sheet turned into a real length, or printed inches marked NTS in an NTS area (`Canvas.measure`, `Sheet.area(at:)`). A length with either point in an NTS area or on an NTS sheet reads NTS first, as before; otherwise the first point's scale area wins, then the second's, the topmost sheet first (since #60, only the topmost sheet under a point: see the amendment below). Ink and other sheets over a scale area are not at its real size; that is the price of keeping the sheet whole. Cutting each detail out as a sheet of its own (question 2, B) was considered and left for later.
- **Reading the scale.** Only lines of the page's text that say SCALE, NTS or NOT TO SCALE are read (`SheetReading`, in `VellumCore`), so a dimension's text is never taken for a scale. The title block's is the one nearest the bottom-right corner; the rest are details'. A detail's area is Vellum's guess from where its scale line sits, since details are titled underneath: from the line up to the next detail's title above (or the top margin) and across to halfway to the details either side (or the margins, stopping short of the title block). Vellum asks for a scale, offering the ones written on the sheet first, for AS NOTED, several scales, no scale, a scale it can't read, and a scan; it starts on the main scale where it has one, and on nothing for no scale or a scan.
- **Placing.** After the confirm step the view zooms out and a true-size outline of the sheet waits in the middle of the view; the Pencil (or a finger drawing, with Draw with finger on) moves it and lifting places it, and a finger's tap places it where it lands. Panning and pinching meanwhile keep the outline where it is on screen. Moving a placed sheet and calibrating a half-size print from a known dimension are not part of this.

## Amendment (#35): a sheet's labels are fitted, never cut

Zach's picks from the prototype at the tag `prototype/sheet-labels`. A scale area's label sits inside the area's top-left corner at full size: its name over its scale, or its scale alone if the two lines don't fit, or only the outline if the scale doesn't, so part of a scale never shows (`SheetLabelLayout`, in `VellumCore`; the app measures the text). A sheet's name sits on a small backing inside the sheet's top-left corner, moving with the sheet, so it can pass behind the top bar and no longer matches a frame's label. A sheet narrower or shorter than its name hides the name rather than clip it. Where a scale area starts in that corner, its label drops below the sheet's name inside the area, with the same fallbacks; an area's label drops below an earlier area's in the same way, so no label covers another.

## Amendment (#44): a sheet turns, moves and changes scale after placing

Zach's picks from the prototype at the tag `prototype/sheet-rotate`, opened with `?q1=AC&q2=B&q3=B&q4=A&q5=A`. "Moving a placed sheet" was left out of #11; this brings it in.

- **What a sheet is now.** Its `origin` stays its corner before it is turned, and an `angle` turns it about its centre, counter-clockwise on screen as AutoCAD reads angles. `Sheet.canvasPoint(at:)`, `paperPoint(at:)`, `contains`, `area(at:)` and so `Canvas.measure` all go through the angle, so a length is the printed length it spans at whatever scale reads there, at any angle: a printed 24'-0" reads 24'-0" at any angle, a scale area reads its own scale, and NTS stays marked. A quarter turn is exact to the last bit. Marks are kept on the ink grid, so a turn or a scale change can leave a point that lay exactly on an edge a fraction of a step past it: a point within one ink-grid step of a sheet's or scale area's edge counts as on it, so a mark on an edge keeps its scale or its NTS. Since #49 (fixed with #57, which snaps to a sheet's edge) the allowance is 1/1024 inch of the sheet's paper, 64 ink-grid steps of paper (`Sheet.edgeAllowance`), and the same allowance decides which markups come along when the sheet turns, moves or changes scale. Each turn rounds the mark again on the canvas, by at most half a step, which on the paper is half a step over the scale, so drift is largest at full size and NTS; a scale change scales the drift with the canvas, so on the paper it stays what it was. An allowance kept on the canvas instead failed after a scale change: 1,000 turns at 1:48 and a change to 1:1200 left a mark 334 canvas steps out. Replaying the rounding over 40 seeds of 1,000 turns, a change among 1:1, 1:48, 1:50 and 1:1200, and 1,000 more, the worst point was 31 steps of paper out. A point a hundredth of a printed inch outside still reads outside at every scale; on the canvas the allowance is 1/1024 inch at full size and 1.17 inches at 1:1200. `bounds` is the box square to the canvas round the sheet, for culling and zoom to everything, and `size` is how big it is.
- **Any angle, catching at the quarter turns.** A twist turns it freely, to a tenth of a degree, and pulls onto 0°, 90°, 180° or 270° within 3° (`SheetAngle.shaped`). There is no typed angle, so an exact angle other than a quarter turn can't be set.
- **Moving and changing scale.** Dragging moves it. Resizing means picking another drawing scale; the sheet takes the size that scale gives it, about its centre. There is no free resize, because one would make every length on it wrong.
- **A scale change reads the page again.** Scale areas are the details at scales other than the main one (#11), so after a change of main scale a detail that was at the old main scale becomes an area of its own, and one at the new main scale stops being one. A page that can't be read keeps the areas it has. Each area's lengths read the area's own scale throughout. Everywhere else, lengths read what the new scale says.
- **An NTS sheet can change scale, both ways.** A scale change on an NTS sheet gives it that drawing scale: it takes that scale's size and its lengths read real from then on. Picking NTS on a scaled sheet takes it to its printed size, with its lengths marked NTS. NTS is one more scale on the list because a sheet confirmed wrongly at Add sheet should not have to be placed again.
- **Markups that come along** (CONTEXT.md) are chosen at the start of each change and go by the same motion (`SheetMotion`). In a scale change they scale about the sheet's centre by the ratio of the scales, widths, letters and dimension text included, so each stays on the same spot of the paper; a dimension that comes along therefore reads what the new scale says. A dimension's horizontal or vertical reading turns with the sheet (`axisAngle`, as AutoCAD's rotated linear dimension does), so it keeps measuring along the paper. Frames stay where they are, square to the canvas.
- **Sending and exporting a turned sheet.** Neither exists yet (Send is disabled until jobs are built). When they come, a sheet goes as it lies: a frame or window takes in what the canvas shows there, turned as it is; a sheet sent on its own goes as its upright PDF page with its angle and origin beside it, since the PDF is never rewritten and the ink log's lengths don't depend on the angle. A redline applied on the laptop reads the markups through `Sheet.paperPoint(at:)`, which undoes the angle.

## Amendment (#60): under overlapping sheets, a scale area reads only on the topmost sheet

Zach's answer on #60, settling the two exceptions to "top sheet wins" that #11 left.

- **Kept: NTS reads through.** A length with a point on an NTS sheet, or in an NTS area, reads printed inches marked NTS even where a scaled sheet lies on top of it, as before. A 1/4" plan covering part of a loop drawing still reads NTS there: a real length read off a sheet that is not to scale would be wrong, and a false NTS is only cautious.
- **Dropped: a lower sheet's scale area no longer reads under a sheet on top.** For scale areas only the topmost sheet containing a point counts: its area if the point is in one, otherwise the sheet's own scale, which is a real length (`Canvas.measure`). A 1/4" = 1'-0" plan with no area there, lying over a lower sheet's 1" = 1'-0" detail, measures at the plan's own 1/4": 120 canvas inches read 10'-0", where before #60 they read the detail's 2'-6". What Zach sees on top is what he measures. A point on the lower sheet clear of the top sheet reads its area as before, and "on the sheet" still includes `Sheet.edgeAllowance` (#57).
- **A length between the two.** The rule for a length is unchanged from #11: the first point's area wins, then the second's. A length from a point on the top sheet with no area there to a point on a lower sheet's area alone reads that area's scale whichever way round it is taken, as a length from outside an area into it does on a single sheet; the top sheet's point has no area to offer, so the detail sets the scale.
- **Dimensions already on a canvas.** One changes its reading where the scale area it read was a lower sheet's, under a sheet on top at that point: it now reads the other point's area, if that point is in one on its topmost sheet, and otherwise the real length. That takes in a mark on an edge two sheets share, which is on both (`Sheet.edgeAllowance`) and so reads the one on top. Nothing on the canvas moves; only the text changes, since a dimension's reading is worked out whenever it is drawn.

## Amendment (#69): placing from the drawing viewer

A drawing placed from the Drawings tab's search is confirmed as one picked in Add sheet is: the viewer opens Add sheet's own confirm step on the page shown, prefilled with the scale the drawing index read off the title block, so a right scale is one tap and Change scale picks another. AS NOTED, or index text Vellum can't read whole, asks for a scale to be picked or typed. The sheet is built by the same `SheetReading.sheet(placedAt:page:invertsBadly:)`, so its size and scale areas are the same whichever way it came.

## Amendment (#94): a fitted plot has nothing to confirm but NTS

A plot the laptop fitted to the page, not to scale (#92), is not drawn at any scale, so Zach has none to confirm. Add sheet's confirm step says "Plotted to fit, not to scale, so it goes on NTS." with its printed size, and offers only Cancel and Place sheet: no Change scale and no scale to pick, whether it was reached from search's Place on a canvas, Open in a new canvas or Add sheet's own Dropbox browser, which knows the plot by its path in the drawing index or the plots on the Duo. It is placed as an NTS sheet at its printed size with no scale areas, since the scales written on its details are no truer than its title block's (`SheetReading.sheet(placedAt:page:invertsBadly:fitted:)`).


## Amendment (#96): a placed fitted plot stays NTS

Zach's answer on #96: lock it. A placed fitted plot keeps its mark (`Sheet.fitted`), written in the ink log as bit 2 of the sheet's flags byte, so it costs no byte and a canvas without one is byte for byte what it was; it travels through the snapshot, sync and a relaunch. Its strip (ADR-0011) has no Change scale, and the ink session refuses any change that would give it a scale or scale areas, from the strip, from sync or from a log, so undo never brings one back. Lengths on it are always printed lengths marked NTS. It still turns, moves and erases as any sheet does.
