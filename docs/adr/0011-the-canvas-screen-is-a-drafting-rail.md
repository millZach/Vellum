# The canvas screen is a drafting rail, in light and dark

Zach picked the drafting rail from three canvas-screen designs (drafting rail, a floating pen palette, and an AutoCAD-style ribbon with a sheet set), kept at the tag `prototype/canvas-screen`. The tools run down a rail on the left: pen, highlighter, eraser, line and shape, then lasso and text box, then measure, dimension, frame and window, with the four markup colours at its foot. A bar across the top holds the canvas name, its units, light or dark, and undo, with Add sheet, Record and Send at the right. A status bar along the bottom shows the pen's position in canvas units, a scale bar, what the current tool expects next, the sheet and frame count, zoom to everything, and whether the canvas is saved and synced. The iOS tab rail at the right edge holds Canvases, Drawings, Jobs and Meetings. The Pencil draws and fingers pan and pinch.

Vellum follows the Duo's light or dark setting, and its settings can pin it to Light, Dark or System, as Sotto's iPhone client does. In dark mode the canvas and chrome go dark and sheets are inverted to light linework on dark, the way AutoCAD shows model space; a sheet that inverts badly (a coloured or scanned PDF) can be shown dimmed instead. Sketch ink and dimensions flip between dark and light to stay readable over what is under them, like AutoCAD's colour 7. Red, green and blue ink keep their meaning in both modes.

## Considered options

- **A floating pen palette** gave the most drawing room but hid most tools behind a More button.
- **A ribbon with a sheet list and a command line** felt most like AutoCAD but left the least drawing area on a 951 × 669 point screen.
