# Ink is drawn by Vellum's own renderer, in model space, not by PencilKit

The canvas zooms from a whole plant, hundreds of feet across the Duo, down to a valve a few inches across (ADR-0002), and every stroke has to stay on the spot of the sheet it was drawn over at any zoom. Each stroke also has to carry its markup colour as a value a job can read (ADR-0009). We considered PencilKit (`PKCanvasView` and `PKDrawing`), which gives low latency, its own eraser and undo, and Scribble for free, and chose a renderer of our own: a UIKit view that reads the Pencil's touches, with their coalesced and predicted samples, keeps every stroke as points in real-world inches (`InkStroke` in `VellumCore`), and draws it through one model-to-screen mapping (`Viewport`) in `Double`.

PencilKit does not fit the zoom. `PKCanvasView` zooms as a `UIScrollView`, and developers report its zoom scale stops at 10 however high `maximumZoomScale` is set ([Apple Developer Forums, thread 125150](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/125150)), where Vellum's range is a factor of 500,000 (0.002 to 1,000 points per inch). Getting past the cap by scaling a `PKCanvasView` inside another scroll view draws its ink blurred, reported from iOS 15 through 17 with no working fix ([thread 698317](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/698317)). Both are developers' reports rather than Apple's documentation, which says nothing on either. Working around both would mean rebasing every stroke's coordinates into a fresh `PKDrawing` as Zach zooms, and keeping a parallel table from PencilKit's strokes to their markup colours, which is the renderer we would be writing anyway, with PencilKit's model in the way of the one that is saved and sent.

## Consequences

- A stroke is a list of `CanvasPoint`s in real-world inches, its pen (pen or highlighter), its `MarkupColour` and its width, all in `VellumCore` and tested on Linux. Nothing about it is stored in screen space.
- A stroke's pen, markup colour and width are fixed when the Pencil touches down (`PenSettings.beginStroke`), so changing colour or pinching with the other hand mid-stroke never changes what is committed. Its width is a real-world length: the chosen line weight at the zoom it touched down at. Ink behaves like ink on paper: handwriting written close up shrinks with its letters when Zach zooms out rather than swelling into a blob, and a redline keeps its width relative to what it marks. Ink is never drawn thinner than a hairline, so it never vanishes.
- The eraser erases whole strokes, and undo and redo cover drawing and erasing, in `InkSession`, and survive a relaunch (ADR-0014). One sweep of the eraser, touchdown to lift, is one undo step, and every sweep is its own.
- Every coalesced sample is ink, the last batch at lift included, so a stroke's tail is the curve Zach drew, not a chord.
- Latency is ours to keep low. If the renderer below is not fast enough on the Duo, a Metal renderer can replace the drawing without touching the model.
- Scribble (handwriting into text) is not free. Text boxes (#7) will need it brought in on their own.
- The zoom stops at 1,000 points per inch, under an inch across the Duo, because that is where 1/16", the finest length the canvas units show, still fits the scale bar. The scale bar only offers lengths its label shows exactly; in decimal feet, closer in than 0.01' fits, it reads 1/16" in inches.

## Amendment (#21): finished ink is cached tiles, and the app runs at 120 Hz

The first renderer redrew every visible stroke, rebuilding its path from its points, on every frame of a pan or pinch and for every new stroke, and the eraser looked at every point of every stroke for every Pencil sample. With 10,000 strokes that cannot hold the Duo's 120 Hz (8.3 ms a frame). We considered `CATiledLayer`, which tiles and draws off the main thread for free, but its levels of detail stop far short of a 500,000-fold zoom, it fades tiles in, and it would need remapping at every range of zoom; and Metal, which would be fastest but is a renderer to write and maintain for a gain the tiles below already reach. We chose tiles of our own:

- **An index of the ink** (`InkIndex`, in `VellumCore`): a stack of uniform grids whose cells double from 1/256 inch, each stroke in one cell of the level its size fits, its bounds worked out once. Culling and the eraser look only at strokes near the place in question, and the eraser compares squared distances and stops at the first segment close enough. `InkIndex.sync(with:)` keeps it in step with the ink after any edit, undo or redo, and reports what changed.
- **Finished ink is drawn into cached 256-point tiles** (`InkTiling`, `InkTileRenderer`) at levels of detail half an octave apart, always at or above the zoom so a tile is never magnified, on two background threads, from each stroke's path built once in model space on those threads (`StrokePathCache`: one build per stroke even when both threads want it, and a stroke erased mid-build is not put back). Each level's tiles sit in one layer whose transform maps them onto the screen, so a pan or a pinch changes one transform and draws nothing. Which tiles exist and which to draw is decided in `VellumCore` (`InkTileBook`), so the work budgets count what the renderer really asks for. Tiles coming into view are drawn as they appear; leaving a level keeps the old one up, scaled, until the new level covers the screen, and a second level change before then keeps whichever drawn level covers (a quick pinch never shows blank canvas where ink was). A new stroke is drawn over the tiles it touches; an erase, undo or redo redraws the tiles it touches, found among the cached tiles rather than every tile position the stroke spans. Until its tiles are drawn, a committed stroke stays on the layers it was drawn on live, moved by one transform, so ink never blinks and the main thread never builds a committed stroke's path. Nothing is drawn in `draw(_:)`: the ground is the layer's colour and the grid is two shape layers.
- **The stroke in progress is chunked** (`LiveStrokeChunks`): full chunks of 64 points are built once, and a Pencil move rebuilds only the open chunk and the predicted tail. It is drawn in screen points of the view at touchdown and moved by one transform for every change of view, so it keeps up with a pan or a pinch without being rebuilt. Its hairline minimum is the one at touchdown until it lifts.
- **ProMotion**: the app opts into frame rates above 60 Hz (`CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone`), and the canvas asks for 120 Hz with a display link only while a finger or the Pencil is down, which also publishes the pen's position to the status bar at most once a frame.

The work each of these saves is a budget in `docs/perf/baselines/canvas-rendering.json`, enforced by `CanvasRenderingBudgetTests`, and the frame times on the made-up 10,000-stroke canvas are recorded in CI (ADR-0015). An erased stroke can stay on screen for the few milliseconds its tiles take to redraw.

## Amendment (#7): snapping, the lasso and text boxes

- **Snapping.** Holding the Pencil still (within 4 screen points) at the end of a pen stroke for half a second (`ShapeSnap.holdInterval`, in `VellumCore`) snaps it to a straight line, a rectangle or an ellipse, a circle when nearly round, shown on the live layers before the Pencil lifts; the line and shape tool also snaps when it lifts, and draws a straight line from end to end when the stroke is no shape. The canvas looks at the hold once a display frame, never per sample, and recognises the stroke once per hold, on 64 points spaced along it (`ShapeSnap`). A snapped stroke is an ordinary stroke whose points are the shape's.
- **The lasso.** A selection is looked up through the ink index (`Lasso`), never the whole canvas. Dragging it takes its strokes out of the index, so their tiles redraw without them, and draws them once on a layer of their own (a shape layer per colour, pen and width), which the drag moves by one transform a frame; the layer stays until the moved strokes' tiles are drawn, so the ink never blinks.
- **Text boxes and Scribble.** Scribble is brought in for text boxes, as this ADR said it would have to be: a text box being typed in is a `UITextView`, which turns the Pencil's writing over it into text by itself, and with the text box tool in hand a `UIIndirectScribbleInteraction` on the canvas places a text box where the Pencil starts writing and sends the writing into it. Text boxes are drawn as UIKit labels over the ink, in model space like it: each is laid out at a zoom and moved and scaled by its transform as the view pans and pinches, laid out again only when the zoom has moved by more than a factor of two or the pinch ends. Letters are laid out no larger than 96 points and scaled past that, so a text box zoomed right in never needs a huge bitmap, at the cost of slightly soft letters there. A text box is not part of the ink tiles.

## Amendment (#8): dimensions, frames and the loupe

- **Dimensions and frames** are drawn by `AnnotationOverlay`, not into the ink tiles: one layer in screen points of a view of the canvas, which a pan or pinch moves with one transform, drawn again only when the zoom has moved by more than a factor of two or the pinch ends, as text boxes are. A dimension's ticks, gaps and text are sized from its text height, a real-world length fixed at the zoom it was placed at (12 points then), so it zooms with the ink; its text is hidden below 4 points on screen. A selection drag moves its dimensions and frames by the drag's transform.
- **Snapping a tap** (`PointSnap`, in `VellumCore`) looks only at the strokes whose bounds come within 20 screen points of the tip, through `InkIndex`, and reads each of those strokes' points once. It runs at touchdown, once a display frame while the tip moves, and at lift, never per Pencil sample.
- **The loupe** shows the tile images the renderer has already drawn for the view, in a few layers of its own under one magnifying transform (`InkTileRenderer.drawnTiles`). It never draws the canvas again, so it is a little soft at three times; a crisper loupe would cost a second render per frame.
- The work these cost are budgets in `docs/perf/baselines/canvas-rendering.json` (strokes compared and points read per tap) and the frame times of a frame drag and of the loupe are recorded by the benchmark.

## Amendment (#11): sheets are drawn in tiles of their own

Placed sheets are drawn under the ink by `SheetRenderer`, the way finished ink is: each sheet's PDF page is drawn into 256-point tiles of its paper at levels of detail half an octave apart (level n draws 2^(n/2) screen points per printed inch, never magnified up to 512, so at most 7 × 5 tiles cover the unfolded Duo), off the main thread on a queue of their own, from the copy of the PDF kept in the canvas folder. Each sheet has a paper layer, shown at once, and one container per level whose transform a pan or pinch changes, so nothing already drawn is drawn again; the last level covering the screen stays up under a new one until the new one's tiles are drawn. Which tiles to draw and which to keep is decided in `VellumCore` (`SheetTileBook`), with the drawn tiles kept up to 96 MB, least recently shown first, so the budgets in `docs/perf/baselines/sheets.json` count the real work. Changing light and dark draws the tiles again, inverted or dimmed. A page's rotation is honoured.

## Amendment (#44): a turn is a transform

A sheet's angle is one more transform, on the layer holding its paper and its levels' tiles; tiles are keyed by the paper, so they keep through a turn, a move and a scale change. While a sheet turns or moves, a frame changes that transform, the transform of one layer holding its lifted strokes (drawn once when it starts, as an image above 200 strokes, as a selection's move does), a position per text box and a transform per dimension that comes along, all in one Core Animation commit, since a second commit in a frame waits on the render server. Labels are built once with both of their fallbacks ready, so a frame only runs `SheetLabelLayout` on the sizes measured then and moves a few layers; no text is measured and no tile or stroke is drawn. Tiles newly in view are asked for every 5° or 64 screen points, and only those the view really touches on the paper, so a turned sheet takes about the tiles an upright one does (`SheetTileBook.keys(for:display:level:visible:)`). What comes along is chosen once, when the change starts, and a stroke's points are read only when its cached bounds cross the sheet's edge. The budgets are in `docs/perf/baselines/sheets.json`, and the benchmark's `sheet_turn` scenario records the frame times.

## Amendment (#40): exact shapes are still ink, and snap points read the ink

**Everything stays ink.** A polyline, an arc and a regular polygon become a stroke's points, as `SnappedShape` does for the shapes before them: an arc 48 segments, a polygon its corners and back to the first. Vellum stores no separate geometry, so a shape can be corrected only while its tag shows, and is ink like any other from then on.

**A correction is an event.** A shape corrected through its tag is `InkEvent.correctStroke`, holding the stroke as it now stands under the same id, and its own undo step: one undo puts the drawn shape back, the next takes it away. It goes to the ink log like any other change, reopens exactly, and reaches Dropbox as the log's tail (ink files version 6, which a canvas saved before it takes on its old log).

**Snap points are read from the points.** `PointSnap` still looks only at the strokes whose bounds come within 20 screen points of the tip, through `InkIndex`, once a display frame and never per Pencil sample. A round stroke is one whose points all lie on a circle: closed with more segments than a polygon can have, or open with exactly an arc's 48. It takes its intersections on the true circle, not its chords, which at a 5-foot radius fall most of a sixteenth inside. A straight stroke has at most 32 segments; only those have mids and perpendiculars, so a hand-drawn stroke's many samples don't. Intersections are looked for only among the segments within reach of the tip and no further than the best point so far, swept along x, at most 128 of them, the latest strokes first; a place too crowded for that is a place to zoom in. Hold-still cycling was not picked (question 3 is A), so holding still does nothing to a snap point.

**A shape being drawn is a few layers.** Its preview, the angle guide and its placed points are three shape layers placed once a frame while the tip moves, at most 73 points. Only when it is finished does it go through the live layers to the renderer, as a stroke does, so it never blinks. The budget for the lookup on a crowded canvas is in `docs/perf/baselines/canvas-rendering.json`, and the benchmark's `shape` scenario times the frame.
